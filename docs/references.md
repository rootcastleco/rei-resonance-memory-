# References and prior art

## Mechanical source references

The design draws on historical engineering principles. No source book body, catalogue image, supplier CAD or private file identifier is distributed.

- Robert O. Parmley, *Illustrated Sourcebook of Mechanical Components*, PDF p. 513 (printed section 16-51): cantilever spring behaviour and natural-frequency relationships. The equations in this repository are independently derived with a static assumed shape.
- Neil Sclater and Nicholas P. Chironis, *Mechanisms and Mechanical Devices Sourcebook*, 4th ed., PDF p. 209 (printed p. 200): one-direction ratchet drive with controlled indexing. The proposed leaky accumulator requires a different return arrangement.
- *McMaster-Carr Catalog 118*, p. 1201: dampers and adjustable-speed controls. Historical miniature rotary dampers on this page list low cycling rates (10 or 12 cycles/min), so direct attachment to a 25 Hz reed is rejected. This is not current purchasing evidence.

## Preliminary prior-art screen

Tuned vibrating reeds are established technology; do not claim that resonance-based frequency discrimination itself is new.

- [US1028488A — Magnetic speed-indicator](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1028488A/en), 1912: resonant metallic elements used as a visual speed indicator.
- [US2424864A — Vibration responsive apparatus](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2424864A/en), 1947: tuned reed frequency/amplitude indication and sensitivity to tuning.
- [US2447191A — Vibrating reed frequency meter](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2447191A/en), 1948: adjustable frequency response of a vibrating reed.
- [US5415026A — Vibration warning device including mercury wetted reed gauge switches](https://patents.justia.com/patent/5415026), 1995: critical-frequency vibration warning for rotating machinery using electrical switches.

Research question: whether a purely mechanical, frequency-selective, leaky event integrator with independent persistent flag can be made practical at low available work. The combination is a design hypothesis, not a novelty or freedom-to-operate opinion. This preliminary search is incomplete. Public disclosure may limit patent options in some jurisdictions.
